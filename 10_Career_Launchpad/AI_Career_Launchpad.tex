\section*{End-of-course skill outcome}
\begin{outcome}[By the end of 26UAMPC204 you can \dots]\begin{enumerate}
\item Turn an open-ended problem into a precise AI specification (PEAS, states, actions, costs, goals).
\item Choose and implement the right search, optimisation or game-playing algorithm and analyse its complexity.
\item Encode domain knowledge as logic, rules or a knowledge graph and explain every inference.
\item Reason with probabilities, build a Bayesian network and plan under uncertainty.
\item Train, evaluate and interpret a learning model, and audit it for fairness and safety.
\item Build and demo an expert system or chatbot end to end, with documentation on GitHub.
\item Pitch an AI solution at a hackathon and turn an idea into a research proposal.
\end{enumerate}\end{outcome}

\begin{keypoint}[Hackathon playbook]
Form a team in week 1 with mixed skills (algorithms, ML, UI, pitching) $\rightarrow$ formulate the problem with PEAS $\rightarrow$ build the simplest working baseline first $\rightarrow$ add one AI technique that clearly improves it $\rightarrow$ rehearse a 3-minute demo with numbers $\rightarrow$ publish the repository with a README.
\end{keypoint}

\begin{caution}[Avoid predatory venues]
Check that a journal is indexed in Scopus or Web of Science (or listed in UGC-CARE where required), never pay to publish without a real peer review, and be wary of unsolicited e-mail invitations promising fast acceptance.
\end{caution}
\begin{keypoint}[A realistic student publication path]
Course project $\rightarrow$ workshop paper or student track (AAAI student abstracts, ACL student research workshop, CODS-COMAD young researchers) $\rightarrow$ conference main track or IEEE Access $\rightarrow$ Q1 journal with your supervisor.
\end{keypoint}
